### **Q1. What is a PRD?**

```text
A Product Requirements Document (PRD) is a detailed document that defines what needs to be built, why it should be built, and how success will be measured. It acts as a single source of truth for the product team by clearly outlining the product goals, customer problems, functional and non-functional requirements, user stories, acceptance criteria, and success metrics. A well-written PRD ensures that all stakeholders have a shared understanding of the product before development begins.
```

---

### **Q2. Why do Product Managers write PRDs?**

```text
Product Managers write PRDs to align cross-functional teams around a common understanding of the product. A PRD clearly communicates the customer problem, product objectives, requirements, priorities, and expected outcomes. It reduces ambiguity, prevents miscommunication, improves collaboration between Product, Engineering, Design, and QA teams, and ensures the product is built according to business goals and user needs.
```

---

### **Q3. Who reads a PRD?**

```text
A PRD is read by everyone involved in building and delivering the product, including Product Managers, Software Engineers, Engineering Managers, UI/UX Designers, QA/Test Engineers, Data Scientists (for AI products), Business Stakeholders, Customer Success teams, and Project Managers. It ensures every stakeholder understands their responsibilities and the overall product vision.
```

---

### **Q4. What happens when a PRD is unclear?**

```text
An unclear PRD leads to confusion, misaligned expectations, incorrect feature implementation, increased development time, unnecessary rework, delays, and higher development costs. Teams may build the wrong solution or miss important customer requirements, ultimately resulting in a product that fails to deliver value to users or achieve business objectives.
```

---

### **Q5. What makes a good PRD?**

```text
A good PRD is clear, concise, customer-focused, and easy to understand. It clearly defines the problem being solved, product goals, user requirements, functional and non-functional requirements, user stories, acceptance criteria, success metrics, assumptions, and dependencies. A strong PRD provides enough detail to guide development while remaining focused on delivering customer and business value.
```

---

### **Q6. Why are PRDs especially important for AI products?**

```text
PRDs are especially important for AI products because AI systems involve additional complexity, uncertainty, and ethical considerations. An AI PRD clearly defines the customer problem, the role of AI in the solution, expected inputs and outputs, success metrics, model limitations, edge cases, human-in-the-loop requirements, privacy considerations, and responsible AI guidelines. This helps teams build AI products that are reliable, trustworthy, measurable, and aligned with customer needs and business objectives.
```
