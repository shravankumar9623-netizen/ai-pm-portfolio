# Day 47 - Reflection

## What I learned

I learned that portfolio architecture is not mainly a folder-organization problem. It is a claim-control system. A recruiter sees sentences and metrics; I need to know which source supports each one, what kind of evidence that source represents, and which conclusions it does not support.

The most useful distinction today was between evidence of work and evidence of outcome. A PRD proves that product requirements were documented. An architecture diagram proves that a system was designed. Neither proves that the system was implemented, adopted, or effective. Likewise, completed analytics exercises prove structured product reasoning, but their simulated metrics are not professional achievements.

## How my product view changed

Before this exercise, I could treat a strong case-study narrative as the final output. I now see the narrative as the last layer of an evidence chain:

> source -> classification -> defensible claim -> product decision -> public presentation

If the source is missing, confidential, simulated, or conceptual, the claim must change. This is especially important for AI products because model quality, user behaviour, workflow value, and business outcome are different layers. Improvement at one layer cannot be assumed at the next.

## Worked reflection on a claim

The simulated 120-to-65-minute change was tempting because it makes the flagship sound outcome-driven. I rejected it as a public achievement. The correct use is a labelled PM practice scenario that demonstrates how I would reason about speed, quality, acceptance, regeneration, cost, and rework.

The evidence-safe alternative focuses on what is real: I documented a faculty-first workflow, proposed source grounding, kept human approval at a trust-sensitive boundary, and designed an evaluation and instrumentation approach. This is less dramatic than a percentage improvement, but it is defensible.

## Decision I can defend

I chose evidence transparency over inflated portfolio impact. The immediate trade-off is weaker headline numbers. The benefit is a portfolio in which every statement can survive follow-up questions about source, denominator, method, and limitations.

For the flagship, the next product action should not be another polished narrative. It should be an evidence-producing milestone: a sanitized implementation or reproducible demo, a versioned evaluation set, an executed rubric, reviewer-decision events, and a measured baseline. That sequence converts conceptual strength into demonstrated product capability.

## AI-specific takeaways

- Model evaluation must be versioned and reproducible.
- Human acceptance is not the same as correctness, and correctness is not the same as workflow value.
- Regeneration and editing can indicate failure, exploration, or both; event definitions need context.
- Time should be measured per approved output, including review and rework, not only model generation.
- Cost and latency must be evaluated alongside quality and user outcomes.
- Human review is a product control, not a substitute for evaluation.
- Confidential evidence can inform decisions without becoming public portfolio content.

## Interview application

My interview story for Day 47 is about judgment under portfolio pressure. I had a plausible outcome statement supported by polished analysis, but its numbers were simulated. I separated the evidence categories, rejected the outcome claim, substituted a precise design claim, and defined the proof needed to revisit the result. This demonstrates product integrity, AI evaluation thinking, risk management, and the ability to turn uncertainty into an execution plan.

## Practical result

Day 47 establishes one truth architecture for the portfolio:

1. The repository-wide evidence register remains the controlling source.
2. Each product claim receives an evidence status and source.
3. Public wording stays within the evidence boundary.
4. Simulated learning remains clearly labelled.
5. Confidential evidence remains sanitized and permission-dependent.
6. Evidence gaps become product work, not marketing assumptions.

## What I will carry into Day 48

The flagship case study should now be reconstructed around verified decisions and explicit evidence boundaries. I will preserve the strongest supported story: the user problem as documented, the faculty-first review model, proposed grounding, evaluation design, instrumentation, trade-offs, and the exact gap between a well-designed concept and a demonstrated product. I will not fill that gap with invented outcomes.

## Final reflection

The strongest portfolio is not the one with the most claims. It is the one where the reviewer can see how I know what I know. Day 47 gives me a practical standard: classify before publishing, trace before claiming, and design the next evidence-producing action whenever the chain is incomplete.
