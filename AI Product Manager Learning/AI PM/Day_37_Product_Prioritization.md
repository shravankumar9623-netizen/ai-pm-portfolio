# Day 37 — Product Prioritization

## Objective

Choose what to build or test using outcome, evidence, risk, effort and learning value.

Frameworks such as RICE, MoSCoW and impact/effort structure discussion; they do not replace judgement. AI initiatives also require data readiness, evaluation feasibility, inference economics, safety and human-review capacity.

## Flagship prioritization

| Opportunity | Value | Evidence | Risk | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Source-linked review | High | Conceptual workflow support | Low-medium | Must have |
| Failure reason capture | High learning value | Evaluation gap documented | Low | Must have |
| Batch processing | Medium-high | Need unverified | Medium | Pilot after core flow |
| Theme customization | Low-medium | Weak evidence | Low | Defer |
| Autonomous publishing | Uncertain | No supporting evidence | High | Exclude from MVP |

## Exercise and model answer

**Simulated learning data — created for PM practice.** Input preview scores RICE 72, batch upload 24, theme gallery 11.7 and auto-publish 4.5. Prioritize preview and batch upload, but validate the scoring assumptions. Auto-publish is excluded independently because the quality downside is high.

## AI decision drill

Prioritize evaluation and instrumentation alongside the feature. Without them the team cannot learn whether AI output is acceptable or economical.

## Interview questions and answers

Start from the desired outcome, compare value and confidence, expose strategic risk, use scores as inputs and explain what is deliberately not being built.

## Reflection

For the flagship MVP, I deprioritize autonomous publishing and extensive visual themes. Reviewability, source alignment and failure capture provide more value and learning.
