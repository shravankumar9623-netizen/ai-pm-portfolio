\# Day 42 — Customer Discovery Evidence



\## Theme



Customer Discovery → Evidence → Insight → Product Decision



\---



\## Objective



Convert customer discovery conversations into structured evidence

that can support product decisions.



\---



\## 1. Opinion vs Evidence



An opinion describes what someone believes.



Evidence provides observable or measurable support for a conclusion.



Example:



Opinion:



> Video creation is too slow.



Evidence:



> Users spend significant time manually converting lesson material

> into scripts, visuals, narration and scenes.



\---



\## 2. Evidence Hierarchy



Opinion

↓

Claim

↓

Observation

↓

Behavior

↓

Quantified Evidence

↓

Insight

↓

Product Decision



\---



\## 3. What to Capture During Discovery



\### User



Who is experiencing the problem?



\### Context



When does the problem occur?



\### Current Behavior



What does the user actually do?



\### Pain



Where does friction occur?



\### Frequency



How often does the problem occur?



\### Impact



What does the problem cost the user?



\### Workaround



How does the user solve it today?



\### Evidence



What supports the conclusion?



\### Insight



What pattern can be identified?



\---



\## 4. AI PM Evidence



For AI products also investigate:



\- Trust

\- Accuracy expectations

\- Human review

\- Failure tolerance

\- AI interaction

\- Time saved

\- Rework

\- Output quality



\---



\## 5. Feature Request vs Problem



A feature request should not automatically become a requirement.



Example:



User says:



> I want one-click video generation.



Classify this as:



Feature request.



Follow-up questions:



\- What problem does one-click generation solve?

\- Which step takes the most time?

\- What does the user currently do?

\- How often does the problem occur?

\- What happens when the workflow is slow?



\---



\## 6. Evidence Table



| Finding | Type | Evidence | Confidence |

|---|---|---|---|

| Video production takes significant effort | Problem | Workflow observation | Medium |

| Multiple manual steps exist | Observation | Workflow analysis | Medium |

| Users want complete automation | Feature request | Interview statement | Low |

| AI-generated content will be trusted | Assumption | Not validated | Low |

| Script generation is the biggest bottleneck | Hypothesis | Requires measurement | Low |



\---



\## 7. Product Decision Rule



Do not move from discovery to solution simply because

users describe a feature.



Move forward when the problem has sufficient evidence of:



\- Frequency

\- Severity

\- Impact

\- Relevance

\- Existing workaround



\---



\## 8. Key Principle



> Do not turn every user statement into a product requirement.



First identify whether it represents:



\- Opinion

\- Observation

\- Behavior

\- Pain

\- Feature request

\- Evidence

\- Insight



\---



\## 9. AI PM Principle



The objective of customer discovery is not to prove that AI

is useful.



The objective is to determine whether there is a meaningful

problem and whether AI could plausibly improve the outcome.



\---



\## 10. Day 42 Takeaway



Strong product decisions are built on evidence rather than

assumptions.



The PM's job is to convert raw customer information into

structured insights and defensible decisions.
