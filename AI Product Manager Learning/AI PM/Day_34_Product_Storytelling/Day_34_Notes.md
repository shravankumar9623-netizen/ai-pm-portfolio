\# 2. AI PM Concept — Product Storytelling



\## What is Product Storytelling?



Product storytelling is the ability to communicate a product by connecting:



\- The user's problem

\- The target users

\- The proposed solution

\- The expected business value

\- The evidence used to support product decisions



A strong product story follows:



Problem

↓

Users

↓

Solution

↓

Business Impact

↓

Evidence



\---



\## Why Product Storytelling Matters



Product Managers work with different stakeholders, including:



\- Leadership

\- Engineering

\- Design

\- Sales

\- Marketing

\- Customers

\- Subject Matter Experts

\- Academic teams



The PM must be able to explain the same product at different levels of detail.



For example:



\### Executive level



Focus on:



\- Problem

\- Business impact

\- Strategic opportunity

\- Success metrics



\### Engineering level



Focus on:



\- Requirements

\- Architecture

\- Dependencies

\- Constraints

\- Technical trade-offs



\### User level



Focus on:



\- Pain point

\- Experience

\- Value

\- Outcome



\---



\# 3. Product Storytelling Framework



A simple framework for communicating a product is:



\## Problem



What problem exists?



\## Users



Who experiences the problem?



\## Impact



Why does the problem matter?



\## Solution



What are we proposing?



\## Evidence



How do we know the solution is working?

\# 5. AI PM Learning — AI Should Solve a Real Problem



A product should not use AI simply because AI is available.



The PM should first establish:



1\. Is there a meaningful user problem?

2\. Is the problem frequent?

3\. Is the problem expensive or time-consuming?

4\. Can AI perform the task effectively?

5\. Can the quality of AI output be measured?

6\. Is human review required?

7\. Does automation create meaningful user or business value?



For the AI Educational Video Generator, AI is appropriate because the workflow contains tasks involving:



\- Content understanding

\- Information extraction

\- Script generation

\- Speech generation

\- Visual understanding

\- Synchronization

\- Content transformation

\# 6. PM Concept — User Journey Mapping



\## Definition



A User Journey represents the complete experience a user goes through while trying to achieve a specific goal.



A Product Manager uses journey mapping to understand:



\- User goals

\- User actions

\- Touchpoints

\- Pain points

\- Friction

\- Opportunities

\- Product interventions

\- Expected outcomes

\# 13. User Journey vs User Flow



\## User Journey



A User Journey focuses on the overall user experience.



It answers:



> What is the user trying to accomplish, what happens during the journey, where does friction occur, and what outcome does the user want?



\---



\## User Flow



A User Flow focuses on the steps a user takes inside a product.



Example:



Upload PDF

↓

Select Project

↓

Generate

↓

Review

↓

Approve

↓

Export



\---



\## Key Difference



\*\*User Journey = Overall user experience\*\*



\*\*User Flow = Product interaction steps\*\*

\# 19. Important PM Principles



\## Principle 1 — Start With the Problem



Do not start with a feature or technology.



Start by understanding the user's problem.



\---



\## Principle 2 — Understand the Complete Journey



A product may solve one step while another part of the journey remains painful.



Always look at the complete experience.



\---



\## Principle 3 — Identify Friction Before Features



Find where the user struggles before deciding what to build.



\---



\## Principle 4 — Prioritize



Not every pain point deserves a solution immediately.



Consider:



\- Impact

\- Frequency

\- Strategic importance

\- Feasibility



\---



\## Principle 5 — Define Success



Every significant product decision should eventually connect to measurable outcomes.



\---



\## Principle 6 — Separate Targets From Results



A target is what we want to achieve.



A result is what we actually measured.



Never present a target as a validated result.
