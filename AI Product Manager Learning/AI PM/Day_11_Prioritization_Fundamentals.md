# Day 11 — Prioritization Fundamentals

## Objective

Make priority decisions using value, evidence, effort and risk.

## Supplied backlog

**Simulated learning data — created for PM practice.**

| Item | Reach | Impact | Confidence | Effort |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Input preview | 80 | 3 | 0.9 | 3 |
| Auto-publish | 30 | 3 | 0.4 | 8 |
| Batch upload | 60 | 2 | 0.8 | 4 |
| Theme gallery | 50 | 1 | 0.7 | 3 |

RICE-style scores are reach × impact × confidence ÷ effort:

- Input preview: 72
- Batch upload: 24
- Theme gallery: 11.7
- Auto-publish: 4.5

## PM decision

Prioritize preview, then batch upload. Auto-publish is also strategically risky, so its low score reinforces—not solely determines—the decision.

## AI decision drill

Add uncertainty and harm to AI prioritization. A high-reach generative feature should not outrank foundational evaluation and approval controls simply because engagement may increase.

## Interview practice

Explain why frameworks do not replace judgement, how confidence is estimated, when risk overrides score, how dependencies affect order and how to handle an executive request.

## Reflection

The value of prioritization is the explicit trade-off. The score is an input; the accountable PM decision remains a reasoned choice.
