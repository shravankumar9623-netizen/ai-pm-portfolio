# Day 41 — Customer Discovery

## Objective

Practice evidence extraction from interviews while separating behaviour, interpretation and unsupported conclusions.

## Interview objective

Understand how faculty reviewers currently verify and approve generated or manually prepared educational content, where rework occurs and which decisions must remain under human control.

## Interview guide

Ask about the most recent workflow, trigger, inputs, steps, checks, failures, workaround, consequence and existing alternatives. Avoid asking whether the participant “likes AI” or would use a proposed feature.

## Supplied responses

**Simulated learning data — created for PM practice. These are not real customer interviews.**

- Participant A checks every solution against an approved source before sharing.
- Participant B rewrites unclear narration but accepts correct equations.
- Participant C wants batch processing but refuses automatic publishing.
- Participant D reports that missing source identifiers make review slower.
- Participant E prefers a manual spreadsheet for predictable items and assistance for ambiguous classification.

## Evidence extraction

| Observation | Signal | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Source verification precedes sharing | Trust and traceability are core jobs | Medium within simulation |
| Editing differs by failure type | Capture categorized rework | Medium |
| Batch demand coexists with approval need | Throughput must not remove control | Medium |
| Missing identifiers impede review | Source linkage is an opportunity | Low-medium |
| Deterministic and AI paths both matter | Hybrid workflow is preferable | Low-medium |

## Signal versus noise

Repeated workflow behaviour is a stronger signal than a single feature request. “Automatic publishing” is not supported. The sample is simulated, small and role-limited, so no production conclusion is valid.

## Problem statement

Faculty reviewers need a traceable way to assess, correct and approve content because source ambiguity and uncategorized rework can reduce confidence in the final artifact.

## PM decision

Prototype source-linked drafts, structured rejection reasons and explicit approval. Preserve batch workflows and deterministic options. The next real discovery step would validate these behaviours with authorized participants and documented consent.

## Interview questions and model answers

1. **What is discovery evidence?** Observed behaviour, artifacts and consequences—not preference alone.
2. **When is saturation reached?** When additional interviews stop materially changing themes for the defined segment.
3. **How do you avoid leading?** Ask about recent events and current workarounds.
4. **How do you synthesize?** Separate notes, observations, themes, assumptions and decisions.
5. **What is missing here?** Real participants, sampling rationale and validation.

## Reflection

This exercise demonstrates synthesis, not customer validation. I must never present simulated responses as research or convert them into resume outcomes.
