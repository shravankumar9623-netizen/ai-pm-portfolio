# Day 05 — Problem Definition

## Objective

Convert observations into a bounded, testable problem statement.

## Framework

Define the user, situation, unmet need, consequence, evidence and exclusions. Separate the current problem from future solution ideas.

## Worked example

Faculty reviewers producing solution videos need to verify that question, solution, narration and visual steps remain aligned because fragmented manual handoffs create rework and trust risk. Available repository evidence documents the workflow and proposed controls; it does not contain validated interview transcripts or production outcome data.

Out of scope for the first problem definition: autonomous publishing, replacing subject experts and claiming learning-outcome improvement.

## Assumptions

- Review effort is material: unverified.
- Alignment errors occur: plausible but frequency unverified.
- Faculty require final control: supported by documented product decisions.

## PM decision

Frame the MVP around reviewable drafts and structured exceptions. Measure acceptance, edits and failure reasons before considering higher autonomy.

## Exercise and model answer

Scenario data: users abandon onboarding at identity verification, support contacts spike at the same step, and no evidence shows pricing concern.

Answer: define the problem around completing identity verification, not “low conversion.” Investigate errors, comprehension and recovery. Do not prioritize discounts.

## Interview practice

Explain the difference between symptom and cause, how scope is bounded, what evidence is missing, how assumptions are tracked and what would falsify the definition.

## Takeaway

A strong problem definition makes some solutions inappropriate and makes the next evidence-gathering step obvious.
