# Day 49 — Reflection

## What I Learned

AI product management requires understanding economics, not just AI capability.

Every model call, tool call, retrieval operation and agent step can create cost.

## Most Important Learning

Model cost is not the same as product cost.

A cheaper model may create more:

- failures

- retries

- human escalation

- rework

Therefore I should evaluate total cost-to-serve.

## New Concept

Cost per Successful Outcome.

This connects:

AI Cost

→ Success

→ User Value

→ Business Value

## Model Routing

I learned that different requests can use different models.

This can balance:

- quality

- cost

- latency

- risk

## Product Thinking

The largest model is not automatically the best model.

The cheapest model is not automatically the best model.

The best choice depends on the product outcome.

## Current AI Landscape

Recent AI launches demonstrate a movement toward:

- agentic products

- AI that performs work

- data agents

- specialized agents

- autonomous workflows

- secure execution environments

This means AI PMs need to think about both capability and operational economics.

## Key Coach Lesson

The AI feature is not the product.

The outcome is the product.

## Interview Takeaway

When comparing AI models, I should consider:

Quality

Cost

Latency

Success rate

Human escalation

Total cost-to-serve

User value

rather than looking only at inference price.
