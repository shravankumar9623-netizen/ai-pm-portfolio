# Day 14 — Go-to-Market and Launch

## Objective

Design a controlled path from product readiness to adoption.

## Launch plan

For an internal faculty workflow, begin with a small invited cohort and one repeatable job. Position the product around verified control and reduced repetitive handling—not “AI-powered speed.” Provide a sample input, operating guide, error recovery and a visible support owner.

## Simulated launch exercise

**Simulated learning data — created for PM practice.**

Twenty invited users produce: 16 activations, 12 first completed workflows, nine repeat users, three support requests and one critical validation failure. Activation is 80%, first-value completion is 60%, repeat use is 45% and the critical-failure rate is 5% of invited users.

## PM decision

Pause expansion until the validation failure is understood. Continue the pilot for existing users with a documented workaround. A high activation rate does not override a consequential correctness issue.

## Launch stages

Internal alpha validates workflow; closed beta validates usability and operations; phased rollout validates reliability at higher volume. Each stage requires exit criteria for quality, support and rollback.

## Interview questions and answers

GTM aligns segment, positioning, channel and adoption. A rollout is an exposure plan. Readiness includes support and observability. Launch metrics cover activation, value and retention. A PM pauses when guardrails fail.

## Reflection

Launch is a learning system. Operational documentation and recovery paths are part of the product.
