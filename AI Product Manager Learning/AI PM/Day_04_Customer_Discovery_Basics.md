# Day 04 — Customer Discovery Basics

## Objective

Learn to gather behavioural evidence without leading users toward a preferred solution.

## Coach lesson

Ask about the last real occurrence: trigger, steps, workarounds, time, errors and consequence. Avoid “Would you use AI?” because stated enthusiasm does not establish need.

## Interview guide

1. Tell me about the last time you completed this workflow.
2. What triggered it?
3. Which steps required the most judgement?
4. Where did you check or redo work?
5. What happened when the output was wrong or late?
6. Which tools or people were involved?
7. What have you already tried?

## Simulated exercise

**Simulated learning data — created for PM practice.**

Four responses report repeated spreadsheet formatting; three mention correction of column mappings; two mention presentation consistency; one asks for AI summaries. The recurring behaviour supports investigating ingestion and validation. It does not yet justify an AI summary feature.

## PM decision

Test a deterministic import-preview workflow first. Record workflow frequency, correction steps and severity. Interview more varied users before generalizing.

## AI decision drill

Use AI only where inputs are ambiguous, and expose the suggestion for confirmation. Keep arithmetic and rank calculation deterministic.

## Interview practice

Good discovery questions are recent, specific and neutral. A signal repeats across behaviour and consequence; a request is not automatically a need. Discovery ends in a decision or a test, not merely notes.

## Reflection

Discovery reduces uncertainty. Its output is evidence strong enough to change priority, scope or experiment design.
