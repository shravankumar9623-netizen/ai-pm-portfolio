\# Day 55 — Reflection



\## What I Learned



I learned how prompt injection can manipulate AI systems and why agentic systems create a larger security attack surface when they have access to tools, data and autonomous actions.



\## What I Improved



I improved my understanding of least privilege, permission boundaries, blast radius and defense-in-depth.



\## What I Practiced



I applied an agent threat model to the AI Video Solution Generator and classified capabilities as allowed, approval-required or blocked.



\## What I Understand Better Now



I understand that an AI model should not be the final authority over its own permissions.



Security boundaries need to exist around the model through:



\- Permissions

\- Tool controls

\- Validation

\- Human approval

\- Monitoring

\- Auditability



\## What I Am Still Developing



I am still developing deeper threat-modeling skills and learning how to translate security risks into measurable product requirements.



\## Career Application



This strengthens my AI Product Manager profile because agentic AI requires PMs to understand not only capabilities but also permissions, attack surfaces and operational risk.



\## Coach Assessment



My strongest improvement is connecting AI capability with security boundaries rather than treating security as a separate technical topic.



\## Key Takeaway



Capability without permission is not product design.
