\# Day 50 — AI Model Selection \& Capability Benchmarking



\## Objective



Learn how to select AI models based on product requirements rather than simply choosing the most capable model.



\## Core Principle



The best model is not necessarily the most capable model.



The optimal model is the one that satisfies the product's quality, latency, reliability and cost requirements.



\## Model Selection Dimensions



1\. Quality

2\. Cost

3\. Latency

4\. Reliability

5\. Task Fit



\## Capability Threshold



A capability threshold is the minimum acceptable performance level required for a specific product task.



\## Model Portfolio Strategy



Instead of depending on one model, an AI product can maintain a portfolio of models for different tasks.



Example:



\- Simple tasks → cheaper/faster model

\- Medium tasks → balanced model

\- Complex reasoning → stronger model

\- High-risk tasks → strongest model + human review

\- Failures → fallback model



\## Product Principle



Benchmark performance does not automatically equal product performance.



Models should be evaluated against representative product tasks and success criteria.



\## Key Takeaway



Do not optimize for the smartest model.



Optimize for the best product outcome.
