# Day 01 — Product Thinking

## Objective

Learn to frame a product as a measurable change in user behaviour, not a list of features.

## Coach lesson

A product manager starts with an undesirable current state, a user who experiences it and an outcome worth creating. Technology is a constraint or option; it is not the problem statement.

## Worked scenario

Faculty members prepare result presentations by copying ranks and marks from spreadsheets into slides. The observable problem is repeated manual formatting and error checking. The desired outcome is a trustworthy presentation generated from an approved marks file while keeping student data local.

Three possible responses are a better template, a spreadsheet macro or a local application. The decision is to investigate frequency, variability and privacy before choosing one.

## PM decision

Define success as a correct, editable result deck produced with less repetitive handling. Do not claim time saved until timed comparisons exist. Preserve a manual review step because rank and marks errors are consequential.

## Exercise and model answer

Scenario: users request an AI chatbot because support replies are slow. Evidence supplied: 62% of tickets concern five known account actions; 24% require policy judgement; 14% are unclear.

Answer: first improve self-service for the five actions and routing. Use AI only for intent classification or draft assistance, with escalation for policy and ambiguity. The primary outcome is resolved requests, not chatbot sessions.

## Interview practice

1. Feature or product? A feature performs a function; a product repeatedly creates an outcome.
2. First PM question? Who has which problem, how often and with what consequence?
3. Why avoid solution-first framing? It hides cheaper and safer alternatives.
4. What is an outcome? A measurable change in user or business state.
5. What is the risk here? Automating incorrect marks or ranks.

## Reflection

My default must be outcome, evidence and constraints before technology. Product thinking makes the solution defensible.
