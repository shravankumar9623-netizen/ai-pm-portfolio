\# Hypothesis and Validation



\## Product



AI Educational Video Generator



\---



\## Purpose



Translate product opportunities into testable assumptions and hypotheses before committing additional development resources.



\---



\## Product Validation Principle



The product should not be built solely because a solution appears technically feasible.



The team should identify the assumptions that must be true for the product to create meaningful user and business value.



\---



\## Hypothesis Framework



Observation

↓

Assumption

↓

Hypothesis

↓

Experiment

↓

Metric

↓

\---



\## Key Assumptions



\### User Assumptions



1\. Faculty experience meaningful friction in manual educational video production.

2\. Faculty are willing to use AI-assisted production workflows.

3\. Faculty will accept AI-generated content when appropriate review controls are available.



\### Product Assumptions



1\. AI can generate teaching-oriented scripts from appropriate source material.

2\. Automated annotation can reduce manual production effort.

3\. AI narration can reduce narration-production effort.

4\. The system can maintain alignment between narration and visual explanation.



\### Quality Assumptions



1\. Generated explanations can meet predefined educational quality standards.

2\. AI-generated content can be reviewed efficiently.

3\. Quality controls can identify important generation errors before publication.



\### Business Assumptions



1\. Reduced production effort creates meaningful value for the target organization.

2\. The value created by the workflow can justify the operational cost of AI generation.



\---



\## Priority Hypotheses



\### Hypothesis 1 — Script Generation



\*\*Observation\*\*



Manual script preparation requires significant effort.



\*\*Assumption\*\*



AI-assisted script generation can reduce preparation effort without unacceptable quality loss.



\*\*Hypothesis\*\*



If faculty use AI-assisted script generation, median script-preparation time will decrease while instructional quality remains within the predefined acceptable range.



\*\*Metrics\*\*



\- Median preparation time

\- Number of manual edits

\- Quality score

\- Acceptance rate



\*\*Evidence Required\*\*



Comparison between the current manual workflow and an AI-assisted workflow.



\---



\### Hypothesis 2 — Annotation



\*\*Assumption\*\*



Automated annotations can reduce manual production effort.



\*\*Hypothesis\*\*



If annotations are automatically generated from the explanation workflow, the time required to prepare visual annotations will decrease without reducing instructional clarity.



\*\*Metrics\*\*



\- Annotation preparation time

\- Number of corrections

\- Expert quality rating



\---



\### Hypothesis 3 — Narration



\*\*Assumption\*\*



AI-generated narration can reduce narration-production effort.



\*\*Hypothesis\*\*



If AI narration is used, narration production time will decrease while pronunciation, pacing, and naturalness remain acceptable to reviewers.



\*\*Metrics\*\*



\- Narration production time

\- Pronunciation quality

\- Naturalness rating

\- Reviewer acceptance



\---



\### Hypothesis 4 — End-to-End Workflow



\*\*Assumption\*\*



Automation across multiple stages creates greater value than optimizing one isolated step.



\*\*Hypothesis\*\*



If multiple production steps are integrated into a single workflow, total production time will decrease compared with the current manual workflow.



\*\*Metrics\*\*



\- Total production time

\- Human intervention time

\- Number of revisions

\- Final quality score



\---



\### Hypothesis 5 — Trust and Adoption



\*\*Assumption\*\*



Faculty will use AI-generated educational content when appropriate review and control mechanisms are available.



\*\*Hypothesis\*\*



If faculty can review and modify AI-generated content before publication, their willingness to use the system will increase.



\*\*Metrics\*\*



\- Acceptance rate

\- Willingness-to-use score

\- Edit frequency

\- Review completion rate



\---



\## Validation Approach



The initial validation process should combine:



1\. User interviews

2\. Workflow observation

3\. Controlled task comparisons

4\. Expert quality review

5\. Prototype testing

6\. AI evaluation

7\. Production-efficiency measurement



\---



\## Evidence Classification



All claims should be classified as:



\- \[Observed]

\- \[Hypothesis]

\- \[Target]

\- \[Validated]



No hypothesis should be presented as a validated result without supporting evidence.



\---



\## Decision Principle



The purpose of experimentation is not to prove that the product idea is correct.



The purpose is to reduce uncertainty and determine whether the product should:



\- Continue

\- Change

\- Narrow scope

\- Run another experiment

\- Stop the investment



\---



\## Current Status



The hypotheses documented here are product hypotheses.



No hypothesis should be treated as a validated result until the relevant experiment or research has been completed and documented.
